Use Cases / Illustrative program structures

How specialty NIL and incentive coverage works in practice.

Two illustrative examples of how athletic departments use specialty insurance to protect performance-based commitments and roster-level NIL exposure. Numbers shown are representative of the structure of placements in the Lloyd's market — actual terms are determined per program based on athlete contracts, medical underwriting, and Lloyd's review.

Case · 01

Division I men's basketball — performance bonuses

An athletic department wants to offer a high-performing player meaningful upside tied to team and individual success — without exposing the program to the full bonus liability if those targets are hit. Coverage is structured around defined performance triggers: the program pays a fixed premium, and the policy pays the bonuses if and when triggers are met.

  • /Total contracted upside: $425,000 in performance bonuses across team and individual triggers
  • /Total fixed premium: $82,400 — paid up front, regardless of outcome
  • /Outcome: meaningful recruiting and retention leverage at a fraction of the contracted obligation

Case · 02

Division I men's basketball — season-ending injury

A program with significant NIL and revenue-share commitments to its roster wants to protect those obligations against in-season injury. Coverage activates if a covered player sustains an injury or illness that prevents participation in the team's remaining regular season and post-season games — the policy pays the benefit face amount per the schedule, owned by the institution.

  • /Roster covered: 6 players, individually scheduled benefit amounts
  • /Aggregate exposure protected: $2,585,000 across the schedule
  • /Total premium: $153,032 — roughly 5.9% of total face amount
  • /Trigger: total disablement preventing participation in remaining regular season and tournament games
