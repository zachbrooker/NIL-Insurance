Athlete Resources / Financial education curriculum

Educational resources for athletes navigating NIL income.

An NIL contract is the start of a financial life that looks nothing like a typical entry-level career. Income comes early, in unfamiliar amounts, with new requests on it from family, friends, and advisors. Goldman Sachs Asset Management is a co-lead investor in our parent firm, and through that relationship we share a curated curriculum of athlete-specific financial education materials — prepared by Goldman's Sports & Entertainment Solutions group — with clients of our NIL practice.

Curriculum / Modules

Two foundational modules for early-career athletes.

Module · 01

Making Smart Money Moves

A guide for athletes at the beginning of their financial lives. Built around the premise that high income does not automatically translate to lasting wealth — particularly when income arrives early, irregularly, and with substantial deductions before it ever reaches a bank account.

Prepared by Goldman Sachs Sports & Entertainment Solutions · For educational use

01

Define your goals

Identifying what matters most — the values that anchor financial decisions through the inflection points of an athletic career. Frameworks for distinguishing between commitment, protection, and happiness as guiding categories for how money is allocated.

02

Building the right team

Each member of an athlete's financial support system has a defined role: agents, business managers and accountants, financial advisors, attorneys. The module covers how each is compensated, how to vet credentials through tools like FINRA's BrokerCheck, and how athletes act as the CEO of their own team.

03

Spending and saving

Why athlete pay follows a unique cycle, why a multi-million-dollar contract translates to far less take-home pay than the headline number suggests, and how to build a spending plan around discretionary income. Covers cash management — near-term, medium-term, and long-term — and the building blocks of saving as the first step toward wealth creation.

L

Listen

Allow the other person to begin the conversation. Ask questions, gather context, and ask for time before responding — even if the answer is already clear. Creating space for a respectful dialogue establishes the foundation for any boundary that follows.

L

Learn

After the initial conversation, work through the request with an advisor. Consider how it fits within personal financial goals, prior history with the requester, and the broader pattern of obligations. Reflect honestly on the emotional weight of the ask.

C

Communicate

Build a script. Choose the right setting. State the decision, the reasoning behind it, what's expected next time, and what — if anything — is being asked in return. Sample scripts cover the most common scenarios: family support, nonprofit solicitations, investor pitches, and breaking long-standing "yes" patterns.

Module · 02

The Art of Setting Wealth Boundaries

Effectively managing requests and expectations from family, friends, and communities. A practical framework — Listen, Learn, Communicate — for athletes who find themselves fielding financial requests from people they care about, with sample scripts for each common scenario.

Prepared by Goldman Sachs · For educational use
